## My Journey: From Shy to Builder

### **The Turning Point (2023-2024)**

I wasn't always confident or technical. In fact, 2023 found me shy, under-confident, and struggling to speak English properly.

Everything changed when I got my first laptop in 11th grade:
- i3 2nd Gen processor
- 2 GB RAM
- 128 GB SSD

To most people, that's an old machine. To me, it was a supercomputer.

Around the same time, I became obsessed with **Tony Stark** from Iron Man. Not because of the style or confidence alone, but because of his **mindset**:

> *He was never perfect, but he was always building.*
> 
> Broken? Build. Scared? Build. Alone? Still build.

That became my private rule.

### **Learning to Build**

In September 2023, I learned Python in three days and tried to build my own JARVIS. The code was messy. Most of it failed at first. 

But here's the thing: **I felt excited instead of ashamed.**

For the first time, I didn't feel like a weak student trying to survive.

I felt like a **builder**.

That was my first taste of what I now call **builder energy**:
- Staying calm under pressure
- Turning pain into work
- Using skill as confidence
- Speaking less, improving more

It wasn't an attitude. It was discipline disguised as obsession.

### **The Fall**

Life tested me hard.

Personal struggles in school knocked me down. By January 2024, even my academics—which used to be my strength—started failing. I scored only **73% in 11th** instead of my usual 95+.

I was spiraling. Emotionally attached to the wrong things. Chasing people instead of growth. And I knew I didn't like who I was becoming.

### **The Real Transformation**

One day, sitting alone after everything fell apart, I had a realization:

> *"That's not the man I want to become."*

That day, I made a decision: **I would stop chasing and start building again.**

I deleted Instagram. I blocked distractions. I created a ruthless self-improvement plan:

- Learned manipulation awareness
- Worked on communication skills
- Improved my appearance
- Practiced public speaking (something I was terrified of)
- Built a strict daily routine

**The work was unglamorous.** I practiced alone in my room for months. But the discipline was real.

### **The Comeback**

Within a month, people started noticing.

Classmates who bullied me didn't know how to handle the new me. Friends who had left came back. Teachers started paying attention. 

But more importantly, **I started getting attention for the right reasons**: my skills, my presence, my ability to build things and communicate ideas.

For the first time in my life, I felt **positive, stable, and in control.**

## What I Believe

My journey taught me a few non-negotiable truths:

### **1. Confidence is Built, Not Borrowed**
You don't wake up confident. You build it through small wins, practice, and discipline. Every speech I give today was practiced alone in my room first.

### **2. Discipline is Disguised Obsession**
What looks like discipline to others is just obsession that's been refined. I don't follow a routine because I'm forced to. I follow it because building is an obsession.

### **3. Failure is Data, Not Destiny**
Scoring 73% after consistently scoring 95+ hurt. But it became fuel, not an ending.

### **4. Growth Requires Isolation**
Sometimes you need to step back from everything—people, apps, validation—to grow. The best version of me was built in the silence when no one was watching.

### **5. The Man You Want to Become Matters More Than Your Current State**
I didn't become better because it was easy. I became better because I refused to stay the person I didn't want to be.
